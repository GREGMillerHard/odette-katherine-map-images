03 / Mānuka Honey

Mānuka Honey
A biological powerhouse

The 1,000 mg MGO 850+ Mānuka Honey Crystals in HONIORA are a concentrated daily dose of very high MGO goodness, efficient bioactives and significantly reduced honey sugars.

i

The compound behind the gradeMethylglyoxal (MGO)

Methylglyoxal is the compound MGO gradings actually measure, and the one behind Mānuka honey's antibacterial reputation. At MGO 850+, HONIORA carries a materially higher concentration than the general table honey the grading system was built to distinguish it from.
Supporting research PMID 18210383 ↗

ii

An antioxidant found nowhere elseLeptosperin

Most of Mānuka honey's antioxidant content comes from leptosperin, a compound unique to Mānuka nectar and largely absent from other honey varieties. It works alongside the formula's other antioxidant actives to help the body manage everyday oxidative stress.
Supporting research PMID 31496237 ↗

iii

Feeds your gut biome, without adding extra calories.Prebiotic oligosaccharides

Mānuka honey carries prebiotic oligosaccharides, including panose, which feed beneficial gut bacteria rather than passing through as plain sugar. Most of the body's immune tissue sits in the gut lining, the Fiberest® HF in our Honey crystals matrix and Livaux® support them.
Supporting research PMID 35967810 ↗Gut & immune link PMID 33753873 ↗

The crystals dissolve into your morning glass alongside the balance of the HONIORA active matrix, at the same MGO 850+ dose, every single day.

1839

New Zealand's first honeybees

European honeybees didn't exist here until missionary Mary Bumby brought two hives ashore at the Mangungu Mission Station, Hokianga, in March 1839. Every Mānuka harvest since traces back to bees descended from that one delivery.

1981

Where the science starts

University of Waikato biochemist Peter Molan began investigating Mānuka honey's antiseptic properties in 1981 and identified the non-peroxide antibacterial activity that MGO and UMF grading still measure today.

2–6 weeks

The entire flowering window

Mānuka blooms for only a few weeks each summer, peaking in mid-December, and beekeepers have to move hives into position before it opens. Miss it, and it's a full year until the next one.

800+ years

A tradition older than the honey

Long before honeybees reached New Zealand, Māori boiled Mānuka leaves and bark as a treatment for fever, inhaled the vapour for head colds, and applied it directly to scalds and burns.

Mānuka bushes · East Cape, New Zealand

Acidic in the glass, alkaline in the body. Organic citrates run through the Krebs cycle in the liver and leave bicarbonate residues behind them.

Water with trace minerals and organic acids moves across the intestinal membrane faster than water alone, after a night of respiratory water loss.

Article 01 · Longevity

Six habits for healthy ageing

Habit 1: Walking for vascular and functional health

  • Regular walking creates shear stress against arterial walls, stimulating endothelial cells to produce nitric oxide, which acts as a natural vasodilator.
  • Studies show walking approximately 30 minutes most days over 12 weeks measurably improves arterial stiffness.
  • Walking maintains a reserve of functional leg strength needed for daily activities.

Habit 2: Calibrating nutrition for the ageing body

  • Stomach acid production declines after 60, reducing the absorption of key nutrients like vitamin B12.
  • Older adults experience anabolic resistance and require increased protein intake (approximately 1.0 to 1.2 grams per kilogram of body weight daily) to preserve muscle mass.
  • Thirst signals weaken with age, making intentional hydration critical to avoid chronic mild dehydration.

Habit 3: Protecting the brain through deliberate novelty

  • Neuroplasticity remains responsive after 60 when the brain encounters genuinely new experiences rather than repetitive routines.
  • Activities combining mental and physical coordination, such as learning a musical instrument or acquiring a new craft, provide the highest cognitive benefit.

Habit 4: Resistance training to preserve muscle mass

  • The human body loses 1 to 2 percent of its muscle mass annually without intervention, increasing fall risks.
  • Resistance training under load creates microscopic fibre damage that the body repairs to rebuild strength and thickness.
  • Simple movements like sit-to-stand repetitions and wall push-ups serve as effective starting points.

Habit 5: Social connection as a biological requirement

  • Chronic loneliness activates the body’s stress response, elevating cortisol levels and increasing systemic inflammation.
  • Strong social ties correlate with significantly lower rates of cognitive decline and cardiovascular disease.

Habit 6: Prioritizing deep sleep for cellular maintenance

  • During deep slow-wave sleep, the glymphatic system opens to flush out metabolic waste products like beta-amyloid plaques.
  • Consistently sleeping six hours or less per night increases the risk of later cognitive decline.
  • Morning light exposure, cooler room temperatures, and consistent sleep schedules support better rest.

General information for healthy adults, not medical advice. Speak with your doctor before changing exercise, diet or sleep habits, especially alongside any existing condition or medication.
